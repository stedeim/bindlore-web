Bindlore refuses DRM-locked files on purpose — a converter for rights the user already has

Calgary, Alberta — September 17, 2026

Bindlore does not remove or bypass DRM. The free Chrome extension from Deimira Baidoo is built as a converter for content the user has the necessary rights or permissions to process — DRM-free files they own, public-domain titles, and photos or scans of paper pages they own — not as a DRM workaround or cracker.

When a file is DRM-locked, Bindlore refuses it. That refusal is intentional product policy, stated on the site and in press materials. This policy distinguishes Bindlore’s conversion workflow from DRM removal or circumvention.

The product’s job is downstream of usable rights: take a usable file, keep parsing and OCR on the user’s device, and export a structured skill package (SKILL.md plus references) for Claude, Codex, or similar agents. There is no Bindlore cloud for book content on the core path. Conversion structures source material for compatible agents; it does not guarantee agent quality. Optional AI extras use the user’s own API key, with requests sent from the browser directly to the provider.

“DRM-locked files are refused on purpose,” said Deimira Baidoo. “Converter, not a cracker.”

Product facts

  • DRM-locked files refused by design
  • For content the user has the right to process: owned DRM-free files, public-domain titles, owned paper pages
  • Not a DRM removal or circumvention tool
  • Core conversion runs on-device; no account required
  • Free on the Chrome Web Store
  • Conversion does not guarantee agent quality
